01 Lab 01 · Astrophysics

Explore Planets & Gravity.

Simulates universal gravitation between a central star and orbiting planets, so you can watch Kepler's laws appear on their own.

F = G·m₁m₂/r²E = −G·M·m / 2av_esc = √(2GM/r)

How it works

  1. Each frame, the inverse-square force between the star and every planet is calculated.
  2. The force becomes an acceleration (a = F/m), and the Verlet integrator updates each position and velocity.
  3. Because Verlet conserves energy well, orbits stay stable instead of spiralling away.
  4. Planets closer to the star move faster, which shows Kepler's second and third laws.

What's included

Constant G6.674×10⁻¹¹
IntegratorVerlet
Orbit typeKeplerian ellipses
ConceptsGravitation, Kepler, escape velocity
Try this
  • Increase the star's mass and see how orbital speed changes.
  • Give a planet a speed above escape velocity and watch it leave.
02 Lab 02 · Dynamics

Explore Classical Mechanics.

A block on a surface is pushed by an applied force while kinetic friction pushes back. You see how net force becomes acceleration.

ΣF = m·af_k = μ_k·NF_net = F − f_kKE = ½·m·v²

How it works

  1. The normal force N equals the block's weight on a flat surface.
  2. Kinetic friction f_k = μ_k·N is subtracted from the applied force F.
  3. The remaining net force gives the acceleration a = F_net / m.
  4. Velocity and position are integrated each frame, and kinetic energy is updated.

What's included

Friction μ0.15 – 0.85
Vectors shownForce (F), velocity (v)
ConceptsNewton's laws, friction, work–energy
Try this
  • Raise μ until friction cancels the applied force and the block stops accelerating.
  • Double the mass and compare the acceleration.
03 Lab 03 · Wave Optics

Explore Ray & Wave Optics.

A laser beam hits a mirror, and you watch the reflected ray follow the law of reflection as the angle changes.

θ₁ = θ₂n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂

How it works

  1. A dashed normal line is drawn perpendicular to the mirror surface.
  2. The incident ray strikes the mirror at angle θ₁, measured from the normal.
  3. The reflected ray leaves at an equal angle θ₂ on the other side of the normal.
  4. Photon pulses travel along both rays to show the direction of light.

What's included

Incident angle θ₁Continuous sweep
Reflectance100% specular
Phase shiftπ radians
ConceptsReflection, Snell, photon flux
Try this
  • Compare θ₁ and θ₂ at several angles; they should always match.
  • Think about how Snell's law changes the picture when light enters glass.
04 Lab 04 · Momentum

Explore Elastic Collisions.

Two masses collide head-on. You can check that total momentum is conserved and see how kinetic energy is shared.

p = m·vp_total = Σ mᵢvᵢJ = ∫ F dte = (v₂′ − v₁′) / (v₁ − v₂)

How it works

  1. Each ball starts with a mass and a velocity, giving it momentum p = m·v.
  2. On contact, the new velocities are calculated so total momentum stays the same.
  3. The coefficient of restitution e decides how much kinetic energy survives. At e = 1.0 the collision is perfectly elastic.
  4. The total change in momentum (Δp) stays at 0.00 kg·m/s.

What's included

Restitution e1.0 (elastic)
Total Δp0.00 kg·m/s
ConceptsMomentum, elasticity, impulse
Try this
  • Make one ball much heavier and see which one bounces back.
  • Compare equal masses: they swap velocities in an elastic collision.
05 Lab 05 · Electrodynamics

Explore Magnetic Dipoles & Flux.

A bar magnet shows closed field lines flowing from the North pole to the South pole, and how a moving charge feels a magnetic force.

∇ · B = 0F = q(E + v × B)

How it works

  1. The magnet is modelled as a dipole with a North and a South pole.
  2. Field lines leave North, curve around, and enter South. They never start or end in empty space.
  3. That closed-loop shape is Gauss's law for magnetism: there are no magnetic monopoles, so ∇ · B = 0.
  4. A moving charge feels the Lorentz force, which acts perpendicular to both its velocity and the field.

What's included

Field linesContinuous loops
PolesDipole singularity
ConceptsDipoles, Gauss's law, Lorentz force
Try this
  • Trace one field line from N to S and confirm it closes back through the magnet.
  • Predict the direction of the force on a charge moving across the field.
